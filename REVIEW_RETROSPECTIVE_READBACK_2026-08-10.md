# PART 4 — THE SIX ATTACKS, RUN

## Attack 1 — Goodhart, one level up

The drift modes the calibration set cannot catch, in rough order of arrival:

- **Claims-file self-authorship (immediate).** The writer authors both ends of the measured leg. The named guard — the monthly extraction audit — does not exist yet and is monthly against a nightly pressure. The redraft instruction's fixpoint is prose that *is* its claim; night one already produced a redraft whose headline is the claim sentence. Drift mode: claims get simpler and units get claim-shaped, the score rises, and nothing the calibration set contains can object, because the set is 34 labels from one night of one brief scored against claims as-logged.
- **Difficulty selection (days to weeks).** Story and framing selection drifts toward what transmits — single-driver, concrete, one-actor stories — because units that transmit are held and units that don't get rewritten toward it. No instrument measures the distribution of attempted difficulty. The owner will experience this as the brief getting slightly boring while every number is green, and there is no row for that.
- **Same-family attunement (standing).** Readers, grader, and writer share a model family and (probe-proven) house doctrine. What transmits to a doctrine-soaked Claude reader is not what transmits to a tired human on audio at 6am. The 81%-raw agreement was measured once, on markdown, on one night. Reader-model rotation is named as a guard and has no schedule.
- **Explain-y inflation (the effort axis, admitted unmeasured).** The cheapest way to make a so-what land is to append "this matters because..." — the register gate's process-voice cousin on the comprehension side. Unit-level pass rates cannot see whole-artifact fatigue, and effort has no detector by explicit choice.

How fast: the score saturated in three nights, so the *measured* axis is already done moving; the selection pressures above operate from night one and are invisible to a static calibration set. Detection currently rests on the owner's Saturday ear (weekly, and offline when he travels) and a monthly recalibration that is not yet scheduled. The honest answer to "how fast" is: faster than the monthly guard, slower than the weekly one, and undetectable between them.

## Attack 2 — Is the depth architecture depth, or a proxy with better branding?

It is a well-built instrument for **one** depth failure — the unfinished thought, mechanism-without-meaning — which is the correct target, because it is the owner's actual five-of-six complaint, and the receipts (China/Medicare/Atlassian/AISI, all TRANSMITTED, all rejected) prove phase one is blind to exactly this. The counterfactual test in challenger attack 2 (if the claim were false, would the so_what change?) is the sharpest single idea in Part 12 — it operationalizes *connectedness*, which is a real component of depth.

But the Question Test measures **felt completeness**, and completeness is not depth. A unit can answer every predictable question and be a closed banality; deep units *provoke* questions (12.9 guard 2 concedes this from the other side). The novelty attack checks recurrence against the take-ledger — that is *repetition*, not *non-obviousness*; nothing anywhere measures whether a claim clears the bar of what an informed reader already believed. And the calibration bar cannot expose this gap, because all four sensitivity fixtures are completeness failures — the bar will pass a completeness instrument and the word "depth" will be declared operational. The taxonomy-grows-from-marks rule is right but means the instrument can never *discover* a failure class; OTHER never fires, so new classes wait on the owner tripping over them.

Verdict: real operationalization, wrong name. Call the axis *closure* (or completeness) in the ledger, keep "depth" unclaimed, and the design is honest. Ship it as "depth" and phase two will end the way phase zero's twenty gates did — a green number adjacent to the goal.

## Attack 4 — The owner's role at 365 nights

The marks are correctly designed — optional, ground truth when they arrive, never load-bearing nightly. That part sustains. Three dependencies do not:

- **The paste path.** The only write path to live task bodies is the owner's hands, it has already produced one near-miss (the stale replacement, IMP-145), and it is currently mid-failure: four replacement bodies at root with uneven rule deployment (the weekly body lacks the inversion rule now) and a snapshot that is three days stale. Nothing can mechanically verify what is live.
- **The push/egress path.** The sandbox has zero network egress, so every publish repair waits for his machine. The measured receipt is the Tesla title: a public falsehood identified at 06:18 stayed live a full day-plus because the correction needed a human with network. On a two-week trip, a broken publish is broken for two weeks unless he works from the road.
- **Calibration and drift detection.** Phase-two authority gates on his judgment (correct), so travel freezes phase two — safe. But his ear is also the *only* anti-Goodhart detector (Part 8 guard 1, Part 12.9 guard 6), so travel turns off drift detection while the nightly selection pressures keep running. Two weeks of unwatched drift is recoverable; the problem is nothing will say it happened.

Cheap hedges, in order of value: **(1)** make the paste class mechanically auditable — embed a version marker in each body and have each task print it into its nightly status line; the snapshot directory plus one grep becomes a body-drift detector using files that already exist. **(2)** A travel mode, written down: while the owner's ear is offline, prompts, thresholds, and writer instructions freeze — no 🔴 lands, drift pressure keeps running but the target stops moving with it. **(3)** A second push path (deploy key on a second machine, or a CI job that can be triggered from his phone) so a public falsehood never again waits on one laptop.
