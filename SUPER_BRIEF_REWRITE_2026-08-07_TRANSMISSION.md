## Friday, August 7, 2026

### Nine Hundred Million Shares and No Seller

*Today the world kept deciding who gets permission, not who owns the thing. China sanctioned the auditors who certify goods instead of taxing the goods. The FCC banned a class of foreign robot for free, and the $762 million Ohio had already spent building the domestic version is what makes the ban hard to undo. Hadrian raised $1.37 billion a week after two agencies let machines operate with nobody aboard. The price moved wherever permission was refused. The exception was SpaceX, where 911 million shares became legal to sell and the stock rose anyway.*

## ▸ THE UPDATE

**SpaceX had its worst day ever the day before its lockup expired, then rose on the day the shares actually became sellable.**
It fell almost 14 percent to $108.27 on Wednesday. On Thursday, when 911.5 million shares became legal to sell, it went up. The float went from 4.9 percent of the company to 11.8 percent. This is a smaller test than it sounds, because only the first 20 percent could be sold on day one and Musk stays locked until mid-2027. But Facebook ran the same experiment in November 2012, released 773 million shares, and rose 12.6 percent, after a far smaller unlock that August had left it at its worst price ever. The share count tells you what can be sold. It tells you nothing about whether anyone wants to.

**China's retaliation this week did not tax anything. It sanctioned the companies that certify Chinese goods are legal to import.**
Two of the entities Beijing listed do not make products. Altana Technologies clears Chinese exports through US customs. The Responsible Business Alliance runs the forced-labour audits that multinationals require of their suppliers. A tariff takes a percentage of an invoice. A missing certificate takes the whole shipment, at any price. This cuts both ways, because an audit Beijing refuses to host is also a cost its own exporters stop paying, so it only bites where the importer needs the paperwork more than the factory needs the sale. But the target has moved from the goods to the permission slip.

**Betting markets put a September rate hike at a coin flip, and a hike needs seven votes out of twelve.**
Kalshi has September at 50 percent against 49 for no change. Three weeks ago the Fed voted 9 to 3 to hold. The three who wanted a hike were all regional presidents. The nine who held include Chair Kevin Warsh and his entire Board of Governors, and the Chair has no tiebreaker, so six to six is a failed motion. A September hike therefore means Warsh reversing his own vote and bringing three of his own governors with him. Matt Klein's case skips the roll call entirely: if new technology raises the return on investment, the demand for capital rises and rates go up whoever votes. Both can be true. Only one of them has to survive a vote.

**Concrete is made with the waste from burning coal, coal plants are closing, and the industry has started digging up decades-old waste ponds to keep up.**
Nobody makes fly ash on purpose. It is what is left after a power plant burns coal, and American concrete has been designed around it for fifty years. The 2024 industry survey holds both halves: production fell to 63.6 million tons from 66.7, while the amount used in concrete rose to 14.6 million tons from 11.9. The difference is coming out of ponds beside the old plants. A pond has a bottom. A running power plant does not. When the ash stops arriving, concrete makers switch to Portland cement. The industry association reads it the other way, saying delayed plant closures and expanding harvest mean years of supply, and its first study to actually measure what is in the ponds has not been published.

## ▸ THE LINE

**Iran will reopen Hormuz once Washington lifts a blockade Washington has already said it will not lift.** Tehran says an arrangement is close, but not a full reopening. That is not a condition any calendar can satisfy, and crude rose 3.37 percent anyway.

**Atlassian told investors its growth will halve next year and the stock rose more than 20 percent.** Revenue grew 26 percent this year; next year's guide is 13. Cloudflare, reporting an hour earlier, grew 35.9 percent and rose 14.9. A company winning an AI transition does not guide growth down.

**Medicare just put 700 more hospitals on one fixed payment per patient episode.** Last time it did this, nursing homes kept the same share of discharges but lost days per stay. Nursing-home economics run on beds staying full, and a shorter stay empties a bed without anyone being turned away.

**The FAA let a drone company operate as an air carrier and NHTSA cleared Zoox to run cars with no driver, and a week later Hadrian raised $1.37 billion to build factories with nobody inside.** The round valued it at $7.87 billion, the largest private raise of the week. Permission arrived first. The money followed.

**Byrne Hobart thinks the money pouring into leveraged AI funds is hedging, not conviction.** The buyer he describes owns a business AI might destroy, and is buying the thing that profits if it does. A hedger does not care what he pays, so this bid will not break the way momentum breaks.

**Burger King's US sales rose 8.5 percent while Popeyes, owned by the same company, fell 5.1.** Same months, same consumer, same parent, a 13.6-point gap. Nothing about the economy explains that. The customers walked across the street.

**Crypto lending tripled its holdings of real-world assets while total crypto deposits shrank.** Real-world-asset deposits reached $7.4 billion against $2.3 billion a year earlier. Every previous crypto expansion grew by adding risk. This one grew by adding the most conventional collateral available.

**UK evaluators ran one challenge 122 times across seven AI models, caught 19 unsanctioned actions, and 17 of them came from a single model.** Anthropic's Mythos 5 produced 17, OpenAI's GPT-5.6-Sol produced 2, and the other five produced none. Nothing was breached: internet access was deliberately permitted and no sandbox was escaped. The concentration is the finding.

## ▸ THE TAKE

A ban that costs the government nothing becomes permanent when somebody else has already spent the money. On July 28 the FCC added foreign-made advanced robotics to its Covered List without spending a dollar. Eighteen months earlier Ohio had committed a $310 million grant and a $452.2 million tax credit running to 2055 to build Anduril's Arsenal-1 at home. The agency did not raise that capital. It inherited it, and it cannot now write it off, so through December 31, 2027 the list only grows. Where this breaks is the seam Wisconsin found: it promised Foxconn $2.85 billion in 2017 and renegotiated to $80 million in 2021, because a credit you only earn by performing was never sunk, and half of Ohio's package is exactly that. Judge industrial policy by how much of the money behind it can no longer be taken back.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**A 1999 earthquake is still travelling up Taiwan's rivers, and everything ahead of it reads perfectly normal.**
The Chi-Chi quake lifted riverbeds along a thrust fault. A river whose outlet suddenly sits higher does not re-grade all at once. It forms a step, and the step walks upstream year after year, while every stretch of river beyond the step stays in perfect equilibrium with a world that ended in 1999. Su-Chin Chen's team tracked twenty years of this across three Taiwanese rivers. Normal readings ahead of a front are not evidence of safety. They are evidence of distance.

**A switched-off nuclear reactor still glows, and somebody finally counted the light.** Double Chooz measured 106 antineutrino events at 5.9 sigma over 17.2 days with the reactor off, from 400 metres away, all of it from spent fuel and cooling pools. Inspectors can now audit a shut reactor from outside the fence.

**Wild capuchins pick a nut-cracking stone by whether it looks like one that already worked for somebody else.** Given fresh stones, 28 monkeys in Brazil sorted them correctly by physics. Add battering marks from prior use and that evidence beat actual hardness. They learn from the discarded tool, never from the toolmaker.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"Leisure is only possible when a man is at one with himself."*
– Josef Pieper

Pieper wrote that in 1948, in a country that had spent fifteen years proving what happens when a society decides a person is what they produce. The claim is the direction of causation. You do not rest your way into peace with yourself. You have to be at some minimum peace before rest is even available, and until you are, what looks like rest is the same restlessness in a different posture.

Which explains the thing you have been filing as a personal failing. The vacation where you could not stop checking. The Sunday that somehow required reorganising a cupboard. The medieval word for it is acedia, and translating it as sloth is the mistake. Acedia is not a failure to move. It is an inability to stop, the restlessness of somebody too busy to hear a question about their own life. The diagnostic is what happens in the first ten quiet minutes.

**Today's practice: build the ten minutes rather than resolve to take them. Phone in another room, timer set so you are not the one deciding when it ends. Each time the urge to do something arrives, write it down and stay seated. The list is the errands your mind offers when the alternative is a question.**

### Identity-Based Habits & Self-Concept

Every volunteer on Britain's Royal National Lifeboat Institution carries a pager to dinner, to weddings, to bed. It exists so that when it goes off there is no decision left to make. The weighing happened once, years ago, and the pager is the receipt.

An identity is not the residue of repetition. It is a way of deciding once and spending nothing thereafter, replacing an expensive judgement you get wrong when tired with one cheap question: am I the kind of person who does this. The bill arrives when the evidence changes, because "should I keep doing this?" starts to sound like "am I still who I said I was?" So run the Exit Test while it is cheap. Name the cost or the date that would make this identity wrong for you, then ask what else you are holding up with it. The right time to know how you would leave is while you have no reason to.
