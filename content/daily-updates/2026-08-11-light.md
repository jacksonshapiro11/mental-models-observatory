## ▸ THE UPDATE

**Nvidia lined up more than $500 billion of private capital for AI infrastructure and did not buy a single day off the delivery queue.**
It signed memorandums on Monday with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to finance chips, power and data centres, and Jensen Huang told CNBC the chips are now an investable asset. That turns a capital-formation problem into a securitisation problem, which was never the constraint. Fabrication slots, advanced packaging and grid interconnects are, and none of them accepts a memorandum as an input. The parallel is not 1999 telecom but Chinese solar, debt-financed from 2009 through a wave of failures into Chinese control of nearly every stage by 2024, so a financed overbuild can concentrate a market rather than redistribute it. Solar's ending was state-engineered and nothing is steering this one, so that concentration may never arrive. The constraint stays physical either way.

**The drug replacing antibody infusions will strand the albumin, because both come out of the same litre of plasma and only one of them pays for the donation.**
Immunoglobulin, meaning pooled human antibodies given by infusion, is the largest revenue line in plasma fractionation, and Grifols, the Spanish plasma group, runs more than 300 collection centres to feed it. A litre separates once, in proportions set by what is in the blood, so you cannot make more antibodies without also making the albumin beside them. FcRn blockers work the opposite way, clearing the patient's own antibodies rather than adding more, and a trial switching stable patients off immunoglobulin was due to read out in February. Markets priced the competitive half; the unpriced side is albumin, and the cirrhosis and critical-care patients who have no substitute at all. Grifols is still building collection capacity rather than retiring it, so the drawdown can run slowly enough that nothing visibly breaks. It still runs.

**Four allied militaries holding signed cooperation agreements lost the first move to China in both Indian Ocean crisis games, and nothing about capability explains it.**
Nine former senior officials from Australia, India, the United States and Japan played scenarios their governments have already agreed in writing to handle jointly, and the agreements did not help. One participant's line carries the finding: signed paper does not put jets in the air. Three frictions did the damage and all three are organisational. India's cabinet rules left an operational headquarters needing diplomatic sign-off merely to share ship-tracking data, and routine interaction is largely absent, against roughly 500 American exercises a year with the Philippines. A matrix game rewards decisiveness cheaply, and real escalation charges for a fast first move in a way an exercise cannot. The agreements were still counted before the game and the delay measured during it, and only the second number described the alliance.

**A Delaware judge ordered Verisk to complete the $2.35 billion acquisition it walked away from, and the win is worth sixteen basis points of the price.**
Chancery judge Bonnie David found Verisk, the insurance-data group, had invalidated its own termination through willful conduct, and ordered specific performance plus $3.85 million in costs. A court can order a buyer to use commercially reasonable efforts. It cannot order an antitrust regulator to approve anything. AccuLynx, the roofing-software company, now gets its merger argued before the FTC by a counterparty a judge just found sabotaged that process, the least valuable form of winning in deal law. Hexion was ordered to perform against Huntsman on an identical finding in 2008, and that merger never closed. The deterrent may sit in other boardrooms, which would make the ruling worth more than this recovery. The number a seller should negotiate is still the reverse termination fee, the only certainty it can actually buy.

## ▸ THE TAKE

Which instrument an authority uses is set by where the cost of using it lands, not by which one works. Europe has designated 671 Russian shadow-fleet tankers and Britain 621, Washington has added none since the Biden presidency ended, and only 43 carry all three marks. A European designation binds European ports and costs Europe nothing, while an American one is expensive to America, because its secondary bite forces every foreign bank and charterer to choose between one tanker and the dollar. The call: the pool designated by the EU and UK but not the US stays above 400 through February 28, 2027, with Washington adding fewer than 120. Where it breaks: an instrument left on the table can be working at the table, and nothing visible from outside separates withheld leverage from timidity. Every published metric counts the instruments used, and none counts the one held.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**Primate brains did not get big for thinking. They got big for seeing.**
A Duke palaeontologist and colleagues built virtual casts of the braincases of 137 living and extinct primates, reaching back 56 million years, and published it in Science. The frontal lobe, the region routinely credited with the primate advantage, scaled ordinarily: its relative size is roughly constant from humans down to tree shrews. What accelerated was neocortex growth in exactly the lineages whose optic nerves widened. The part everybody credits was along for the ride, and the part nobody mentions was doing the work.

**Around the age of fifty the brain's memory centre starts quietly swapping out its immune cells for different ones.** A study in Science found hippocampal microglia declining from roughly fifty to seventy-five, replaced by cells carrying stronger inflammatory signatures. Inflammation in an ageing brain may be an arrival rather than a decay.

**The precision of our best measurement of the Earth's interior is set by how well we know the rock next to the detector.** A sphere of scintillator two kilometres under Ontario caught about fifty geoneutrinos, and most of what any detector catches comes from nearby crust. A bigger instrument collects the same contaminated mixture faster.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"Although we know that a frozen pond is entirely water, the sun's heat is necessary to melt it. Although we awaken to the fact that an ordinary man is Buddha, the power of dharma is necessary to make it permeate our cultivation."*
– Chinul, *Secrets on Cultivating the Mind*

A pond in January is water. Every molecule of it. You could stand on it all winter without that changing.

Chinul, in a Korean monastery around 1205, built his argument on that. His contemporaries had split between sudden awakening and lifelong cultivation; he said both, in that order. Understanding arrives all at once, and habits do not follow.

Which fixes a very modern despair. You had the insight, felt the whole thing rearrange, said it out loud. Within a week you were doing the old thing again, and it seemed reasonable to conclude the insight was fake. You have mistaken the ice for the water. Understanding and thawing run on different clocks, and you have only finished the fast one.

**Today's practice: take the one thing you have already understood and keep failing to do, and write down how many times you have practised it since. Not the failures. The reps. Under ten and you have a repetition problem rather than a sincerity problem, which is much better news.**

## ▸ THE MODEL

### Information Cascades & Availability Bias

In 1954 drivers in one American city noticed tiny pits in their windshields, a newspaper put it on the front page, and within two days reports were arriving faster than the police could log them. The pits were road grit, accumulated over years. You normally look through a windshield, not at it.

The mechanism is not stupidity. You find two pits, you know eleven neighbours already reported damage, you reasonably conclude they know something you do not, and you file. Each person adds a conclusion to the public record while keeping their evidence private, so a consensus aggregates decisions rather than information.

Count independent observations, not votes, and ask what single observation would break it. If the answer is one person going and checking, go and check.
