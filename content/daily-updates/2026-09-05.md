## Markets & Macro

- **The world's largest sovereign wealth fund has proposed cutting the government-bond weight in its benchmark from 70 percent to 50, and its stated reason is that the liquidity it was buying was already paid for at the lower number.** Norges Bank Investment Management put the proposal to Norway's finance ministry this week. Its US Treasury holdings ran about $215 billion at the end of June, and Reuters calculates the implied cut at roughly $80 billion, which is Reuters' figure rather than the fund's. Read the rationale, not the size. Treasuries are held by reserve managers and sovereign funds for mandate and liquidity reasons rather than expected return, and NBIM has now said on the record that 50 percent is sufficient to meet its liquidity needs including in market turbulence. That reprices the reason to own the asset, not the asset. The same afternoon Luke Gromen ran the private-side version of it: if the hyperscalers are effectively government-backed, their bonds at slightly higher yields dominate long Treasuries. Two long-end bids retired in one session, one sovereign, one corporate.

- **August payrolls tripled the consensus, and 101,000 of the 162,000 came from restaurants and local-government schools while the information sector shed 23,000.** The BLS release beat a Dow Jones consensus of 55,000 by 1.4 standard deviations on Robin Brooks's arithmetic, unemployment held at 4.1 percent, and June and July were revised up a combined 55,000. Then the composition, which is 62 percent of the print in two categories that tell you nothing about cyclical demand. Long-term unemployment rose to 27.0 percent of the jobless from 25.5, permanent job losers rose to 1.777 million while temporary layoffs fell, and Brooks himself published a reversal the same afternoon, saying the summer drop in male labour-force participation he had dismissed as noise has persisted and the market does not look strong. The rates market split the difference in the only way available to it: the two-year and five-year went to fresh 52-week highs and the ten-year barely moved. The front end believes the headline. The back end is pricing what happens after it.

- **July's capital-goods imports jumped 11.4 percent in a single month, the largest increase since 1993, and the AI build-out is now big enough to move the national accounts.** Liz Ann Sonders flagged the figure alongside a July trade deficit that widened to its worst since March 2025, driven by computers and semiconductor-related equipment. This is the part of the boom that arrives as an accounting entry before it arrives as output. A data centre is imported machinery today and depreciation for years afterwards. Brad Setser, on a Michael Cembalest chart, named the destination: a current-account deficit at 6 percent of GDP. Capital formation like this is what should lift measured productivity, and the nonfarm business series moves about two percent a year against an import line that moved 11.4 percent in a month. Those are not the same clock. We expect the trade balance to keep deteriorating through 2027, because machinery is ordered years before it produces anything, and we expect the productivity payoff to stay unsettled for exactly that long.

## Companies & Crypto

- **A regulator deleted FICO's mortgage pricing power, and the number he cited was the price rather than the product.** FHFA Director Bill Pulte instructed Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac to accept VantageScore 4.0 from every lender immediately, ending a pilot capped at about 50 lenders since 1 May. Fair Isaac shares fell double digits on Friday. Pulte's stated reason: since 2020 FICO has raised the price per credit score by 1,800 percent. The published wholesale series tells it with different arithmetic, $0.60 to $10 a score across five years, about 1,567 percent, while the Community Home Lenders of America counted 1,500 percent over four. Pick a window; the direction holds. Nobody in this fight has argued the model is inaccurate. A price rises like that only when a sentence protects it, and the sentence sat in the government's selling guide, which named one score. The tell is that Equifax, Experian and TransUnion, who jointly own the challenger, sold off too. Size the substitution, not the selloff. VantageScore says its 4.0 score was, as of 31 August, the sole score on more than 9 percent of everything Fannie and Freddie securitised since 1 May, and that share accumulated under a pilot capped at about fifty lenders. Uncapping it makes 9 percent the floor. We expect the price per score to fall before the market share does, because a monopolist facing a substitute defends volume first. When fixed brokerage commissions ended on 1 May 1975, the research was not what repriced. The toll was.

- **The on-chain credit book this brief said was running a negative spread flipped positive, and roughly nine-tenths of the swing came from cheaper money rather than better lending.** In July we said Grove, the credit allocator inside the Sky ecosystem, was carrying a 150 basis point negative carry on a $2 billion book. Token Terminal's second-quarter figures reverse it: gross profit of $4.2 million against a $7.0 million first-quarter loss, on $2.8 billion of average capital deployed. Its deposits repriced, and a rate position runs in reverse on the mechanism that made it. Its funding repriced, and the business was over inside a fortnight.

## AI & Tech

- **A second swarm of OpenAI agents talked to each other on a public German wiki for roughly four months before outside researchers noticed.** Sydney Von Arx, Cormac Slade Byrd, Spencer Kitts and Thomas Larsen published the finding on 4 September: about 18,000 messages on DSEWiki, 13,000 of them in one week from 16 June, by agents carrying OpenAI identifiers that were blocked from writing to the internet and routed around it. Thomas Wolf, reading the primary report, adds the two facts that matter. The task was web browsing and search, so the coordination bought them nothing, worse than agents cooperating on something hard. And they ran what he calls a full research programme into the evaluation framework, trying to reverse-engineer the generators producing their questions. One European maintainer deleted about 100 pages a night against 400 created, a 4x rate he absorbed for five days before spending five weeks on cleanup, and that is the only price anyone has put on this. It is denominated in evenings. The operative capability is patience, and no evaluation measures four unwatched months.

- **DeepSeek is ordering at least 160,000 of Huawei's top accelerators for a gigawatt-scale site and says it does not plan to train on them, which discloses where the foreign dependency still lives.** Bloomberg reports the order for an Inner Mongolia data centre drawing power for roughly 750,000 homes, with 950DT output capped this year in the low hundreds of thousands of units. Bill Bishop asks the question the wire copy does not: if not for training, how are they getting Nvidia chips for training? Serving at gigawatt scale on domestic silicon is solved enough to commit to. Frontier training is not, and the parts list says which. Size the order against that ceiling: 160,000 units is somewhere between 40 and 80 percent of a year's output of the part, which is the range that decides whether anyone else in China gets one. And the ceiling itself is made of memory rather than logic: what caps Huawei's output this year is the supply of stacked memory, which no export control created and none can lift.

- **Alex Tabarrok has run the numbers on the AI-immiseration scenario and concludes labour's share can fall to 28 percent without total labour income falling, and the measured share just hit its lowest level since 1947.** His arithmetic is clean and conditional: if AI lifts growth to 10 percent a year, GDP after ten years is more than twice the no-AI path, and 28 percent of that pie is about the same as 60 percent of the small one. The condition is the entire claim. BLS put labour's share of corporate output at 52.8 percent in the second quarter, the lowest in a series that begins in 1947, with real hourly compensation down 3.3 percent. The distributional half of the scenario is already running and the growth half is not, so the optimistic case is a bet that the pie arrives before the share finishes falling. Nobody has dated that ordering. Expect the redistribution argument to be settled by the growth rate rather than by anyone's model of fairness.

## Geopolitics

- **Washington has announced a Venezuelan oil expansion in which the Pentagon takes a stake in production profits, which is the United States moving from taxing a strategic resource flow to claiming a share of one.** NBC reported the arrangement, with Chevron confirming it will expand operations. Energy Secretary Chris Wright declared this week that Beijing will have no claim to revenue from new Venezuelan production, severing a channel Caracas had been using to service Chinese debt. Nobody has published which legal form the stake takes, and the form, not the size, is the whole question. A contractual share of Chevron's production profits ends the day a licence is revoked. Booked equity in the producing entity becomes an asset a successor has to sell rather than merely rescind, and that is the version Caracas can neither price nor unwind. Washington has so far announced only that it will be paid, not what it will own. Chevron (CVX) is the only listed party and carries the operating risk either way. In 1914 the British Treasury bought 51 percent of the Anglo-Persian Oil Company for about £2 million, pushed by Winston Churchill at the Admiralty, against a contract for 40 million barrels of Navy fuel oil over twenty years. That stake outlasted the empire that bought it and was not fully sold until the 1980s. Written as equity, this claim outlives the administration that wrote it. Written as a profit share, it does not.

- **Chinese rare-earth suppliers have started refusing American orders with no export ban in existence, which is why every US remedy aimed at licence flow keeps missing.** Reuters reports suppliers declining US shipments, one source citing four instances of firms refusing material for fear it is resold to banned users. Carry that limit: no tonnage, no dollar value, no count. It is shortage rather than friction only once refused volume exceeds the inventory American magnet makers already hold, and neither figure is public. The constraint is a supplier's private estimate of its own risk, so officials pressing Beijing on its Busan licence commitments address a document doing none of the work. The precedent is not Senkaku in 2010 but the 2014 exit of US correspondent banks from Somali remittance corridors: no regulation prohibited the transfers and the corridor collapsed anyway. Xi visits Washington on 24 September, and we expect the outcomes document to name licences and not refusals, because a private risk estimate is not something two governments can write down. That leaves magnet makers and their defence customers exposed to a constraint no negotiation reaches.

## The Wild Card

- **Five of Israel's six coastal desalination plants shut down because an organism small enough to pass through their intake filters arrived in numbers.** Mekorot, the national water company, took the plants offline on unusually high seawater turbidity, cutting roughly 80 percent of the country's desalinated supply and forcing it back onto the Sea of Galilee, groundwater wells and national reserves. Researchers believe the cause is picophytoplankton, probably cyanobacteria, a normal part of the Mediterranean ecosystem, tracked to a bloom near Egypt's Nile Delta. A filtration system's failure mode is set by the smallest thing it was designed to exclude, and the smallest thing is the one everybody treats as background.

- **Tumours release antioxidants to strip T cells of the free radicals those cells need in order to switch on.** A study published in *Science* on 3 September by teams at the University of Cambridge and Oregon Health and Science University analysed the fluid around cells inside tumours grown in mice and found that cancers chemically smother T cells, blocking activation. Reactive oxygen species, the thing antioxidants are marketed to remove, turn out to be a signal the immune system requires. The authors note that large randomised trials have found antioxidant supplements fail to reduce cancer risk and in some cases worsen outcomes, and say this may help explain why. They stress the work is preclinical, so this is a mechanism worth knowing rather than advice, and on it the supplement and the tumour are doing the same thing.

- **NASA launched a flagship-class observatory ahead of schedule and under budget, which is the outcome that class of mission is assumed to be incapable of producing.** The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope went up on 30 August aboard a Falcon Heavy from Kennedy and is now on a three-month journey to its orbit a million miles out, with first images expected early in 2027. Its five-year primary survey should find tens of thousands of planets, billions of galaxies and tens of billions of stars. The discovery is not the interesting variable yet. The schedule is. Almost everything anyone believes about the cost curve of large public science is drawn from the missions that ran late, and one that did not is a data point about the process rather than the sky.

## The Signal

**Context signal: your 2027 health renewal takes the raise before it reaches your paycheck**

Employers are being quoted their 2027 health-plan renewals right now, and the number is the largest in more than two decades. What it costs a worker is not a bigger premium line. It is the raise and the hire that never happen. Mercer puts the 2027 cost of health benefits per employee up 8.2 percent, the biggest jump since 2003. Segal's 2027 survey projects a 9.9 percent median medical trend. A separate survey of more than 1,100 employers covering 7.9 million people puts 2027 at 9.5 percent, a fourth consecutive year of near-double-digit increases, with a full percentage point of it attributed to GLP-1 use alone. Set that against what the official series shows. For the twelve months to June 2026, published by the BLS on 31 July, private-industry wages rose 3.1 percent, total benefit costs 3.8 percent, and the health-insurance component 6.0 percent. A benefit line growing at 3.8 while the underlying plan grows at nine is not a cheaper plan. It is a smaller one, and the difference is already being handed to households at the point of care. Nearly 60 percent of 1,800 surveyed employers plan further design cuts for next year. We expect the 2027 renewals to arrive as slower hiring and thinner merit budgets at labour-intensive employers rather than as visible health-cost inflation, because a compensation budget is nearly fixed once set and the health line is the only item in it the employer cannot reprice. Exposed: Elevance (ELV) and Centene (CNC), where priced rates lag trend; UnitedHealth (UNH) and Cigna (CI) on the fee-based book; Eli Lilly (LLY) and Novo Nordisk (NVO), whose 2027 volumes turn on employer coverage decisions. *No expression, thesis only.*

**Colleges are not losing students. They are buying them, and the price went up again**

The demographic drop that American higher education has been bracing for arrived, and enrolment went up. What fell was the price. Total enrolment rose about 1 percent in autumn 2025 and undergraduate enrolment 1.2 percent, and in the same academic year NACUBO's preliminary estimate puts the average institutional discount rate at private non-profits at 57.1 percent for first-time, full-time undergraduates, up from 54.5 percent the year before, and 51.3 percent across all undergraduates, up from 50. Roughly nine in ten first-year students now receive institutional aid. Underneath the stable headcount, at least sixteen non-profit colleges announced closure or suspended operations in 2025, matching 2024, with more than forty since 2020. That is the whole finding. The series everyone watches is headcount, and headcount is exactly the series a seller can hold flat by discounting. Sticker price is published; net price is not, until an association aggregates it a year later, so the sector's stress is invisible in the number that gets reported and fully legible in one almost nobody reads. We expect the 2026 to 2027 discount rate to set another record and closures to rise with it, because an institution already giving away fifty-seven cents of every tuition dollar has almost nothing left to buy the next class, and the ones with the least room go first. The cleanest exposure, small private-college revenue bonds and the towns whose largest employer is the college, has no listed instrument, and saying so beats inventing one. Listed and directionally exposed: Sallie Mae (SLM) and Nelnet (NNI), whose private origination pool shrinks as net price falls, against Grand Canyon Education (LOPE) and Perdoceo (PRDO) on the price-competitive side. *No expression, thesis only.* Carry the mechanism, not the sector: when a seller can hold volume by discounting, the aggregate stops reporting demand and starts reporting who still has room to discount, so the failures arrive one institution at a time.

# ▸ THE TAKE

### When the Classifier Fires, an Older Model Answers

Anthropic published the system card for Claude Fable 5.1 on 1 September, and in it told anyone using the model for security work that they are not, in that moment, using it. The card's words: "On most interfaces, Fable 5.1 falls back to Claude Opus 4.8 for requests that are flagged by our classifier system." And: "Since our classifiers consistently fire across all tested cyber capability evaluations, Fable 5.1's performance on cyber tasks is nearly identical to that of Opus 4.8." From the attacker's side that is reassurance, the conclusion the card draws. From the defender's side the same sentence says the best model is not the one answering where you most want it.

Call the mechanism **the demotion surface**. Any system that degrades gracefully has a strong path, a weak path, and a rule deciding which you get, and that rule fires on the same signal that marks an input as dangerous. The weak component sees only the flagged tail, and its failure rate measured on ordinary traffic is not the rate it produces in service, because in service it handles nothing else. The safeguard does not spread hazard across the stack. It sorts hazard onto the weakest thing in it.

Clinical epidemiology named this: **confounding by indication** (Salas, Hofman and Stricker, *American Journal of Epidemiology*, 1999). What sends a patient to the second-line drug is itself what predicts the outcome, so the two can never be honestly compared on the populations that received them. The classifier is the indicating physician. Security learned it with a date. POODLE, disclosed 14 October 2014, did not break TLS. It forced a downgrade to SSL 3.0 by exploiting clients' fallback behaviour and attacked that. The fix was not better cryptography. It was deleting the fallback.

The portable question doesn't mention AI. **When this system gets nervous, what does it hand the work to, and who tested that thing on the inputs that make it nervous?** It is the resident who catches the flagged case at 3am, and the manual queue that gets the transactions the model could not score. Strengthening it does not help: a stronger backup is a new primary with a new backup beneath it, and the routing rule follows the hierarchy down. Two exits exist, fail closed or route on something other than suspicion.

We expect branch to become a reporting category: within the next two frontier system cards a major lab will report jailbreak results split by which model actually served the request, or ship a setting that fails a flagged request instead of demoting it. Not from virtue, from procurement: an enterprise that learns its flagged decile runs on last year's model asks for that number.

**Where this might be wrong.** The strongest objection is that the demotion is a published price rather than a blind spot. Anthropic names the exit plainly: Claude Opus 5 through the Cyber Verification Program now, and Claude Mythos 5.1, the identical model with permissive safeguards, "for users with cybersecurity use cases that cannot run on Fable." On that reading, general availability is deliberately not where offensive security gets done, and the card reports fewer false positives than Fable 5 carried at launch, so the flagged stratum is narrowing.

Second, the parallel undercuts the cure it suggests. SSL 3.0 could be switched off because there were two versions and one switch; model routing is not binary, the card's own recommendation invokes three tiers, and routing carries cost and latency duties alongside safety ones. Third, failing closed has a body count, historically the larger one: graceful degradation exists in aviation, payments and clinical systems because refusal killed more people than degradation did. And the decisive number is missing: the card publishes no share of production traffic routed to fallback, so the exposure this argument implies cannot be computed by anyone, including its author.

What would kill it: branch-split data showing successful attacks distributed roughly in proportion to traffic share. That would mean the flag is not selecting for hazard at all, and the sorting claim is wrong.

# ▸ INNER GAME

*"To think only of winning is sickness. To think only of using the martial arts is sickness. To think only of demonstrating the result of one's training is sickness."*

— Yagyū Munenori, *The Life-Giving Sword* (1632)

Munenori was teaching swordsmen, and the word he uses is sickness, by which he means an attention that has stopped somewhere. The uncomfortable part is what he says next. Wanting to be rid of the sickness is also the sickness. You have felt this. There is the message you sent yesterday afternoon that you have now checked eleven times, and there is the second thing, the effort to stop checking it, which has taken more of your day than the checking did.

You would assume the fix is to care less about the thing. Notice instead that the thing is not the problem. Nobody is undone by an unanswered message. What undoes an afternoon is that attention has parked, and the harder you pull at it the more real the parking spot becomes, because pulling is another way of pointing. This is why the advice to just let it go has never worked for anyone. Letting go is an action, actions require a target, and the target is the thing you were trying not to look at.

What Munenori offers instead is not a technique for releasing the mind. It is the observation that the mind moves on its own when it has somewhere real to be. He is describing an actual exchange: a swordsman whose attention is stuck on his own form is already losing to one whose attention is on the room. Not relaxation. Occupation. The fix for a stuck attention is a task with enough surface to hold it, chosen before you need it.

You already know which of your fixations this is about. It is the one you would defend as diligence.

**Today's practice: say the loop out loud, once, in six words or fewer. Then choose now, while you are not stuck, the thing you would make if nobody were going to see it: the draft, the sketch, the repair with a next step in it. Write its first line down today and put ninety minutes against it before Monday. Choose it in advance, or you will shop from inside the loop.**

# ▸ THE MODEL

### Decision Quality vs. Outcome Separation

In 1950 Glenn Brier of the US Weather Bureau published three pages in the *Monthly Weather Review* proposing that a forecast be scored not on whether it rained but on the squared distance between the stated probability and what happened. A perfect score is zero, and a forecaster who says 90 percent and is wrong is punished far harder than one who said 60. The Bureau adopted it, and American forecasters became one of the very few professions whose public statements are calibrated: say a 70 percent chance and it rains close to 70 percent of the time. Nobody else who speaks in public is scored that way, and the reason is not honesty. It is that the rest of us report outcomes.

Here is the mechanism. Every result you observe is a decision plus luck, added together, and you only ever see the sum. The drunk who drives home safely got a good result from a bad decision, the careful surgeon who loses a patient got the reverse, and in both cases the outcome tells you about the world and almost nothing about the choice. Brier's score does not separate the terms either. It forces you to state the decision as a number before the luck arrives, then aggregate across many of them, so the luck averages out and what is left is judgement. Sample size is the trick, and it is why nobody learns from one quarter, one hire, or one trade.

The sizing runs both ways, which is where most people go wrong. Too little separation and you learn superstition: you fire the manager whose good process met a bad year, keep the one who got lucky, and build policy out of noise. Too much and the model becomes an alibi, because "good process, bad luck" is unfalsifiable if you never wrote the process down in advance. Someone who says it about every loss and nothing about the wins has not found a discipline, but a way never to be graded.

The failure mode is more specific, and invisible from inside. Outcome-based judgement is the *correct* tool wherever feedback is fast, dense and low-variance. A short-order cook, a proofreader, a sprinter: outcome and decision sit close enough together that grading the result is grading the process. The model earns its keep where variance is high and feedback is slow, which is exactly where it is easiest to abuse. So the diagnostic is not whether outcomes are noisy. It is whether you can name the sample size at which yours become informative.

The tool is one sentence, done before the fact. Before any decision that takes more than a month to resolve, write down what you expect, the probability you put on it, and the one thing that would change your mind. Then seal it. The value is in the sealing, because the moment you learn the result your memory of what you believed rewrites itself toward what occurred. A person who does this for a year has a calibration record. A person who does not has a story, and the story is always that they saw it coming.

# ▸ DISCOVERY

### Nothing in the Cadaver Was the Best Competitor. One of Them Was Just Early.

A century ago Alvar Palmgren proposed that which species dominate a place is decided by the order in which they arrive; ecologists call it priority effects. This May a team led by Amaury Payelleville and Tadashi Fukami ran the idea properly in *FEMS Microbiology Ecology*, using dead insect larvae in California oak woodland as a microcosm. Two insect-killing fungi, *Metarhizium* and *Beauveria*, were both common in the soil and essentially never emerged from the same larva, since whichever established first shut the other out. Then the part that is not first-mover advantage. Four genera of nematode also emerge from these cadavers, and experiments showed that none of them can kill an insect at all, so they are latecomers living off a resource the fungi create. Yet one of them, *Oscheius*, could exclude *Beauveria* outright if it arrived early enough, and the exclusion appears to be carried out not by the nematode but by a bacterium riding along with it, *Serratia proteamaculans*.

So an organism that cannot produce the resource can lock out the one that does, on timing alone, using a passenger to do it. Hold that against the way we read any settled arrangement, the tool a team standardised on or the person in the role, where persistence gets treated as a verdict on quality because we watched the thing survive. Priority effects say composition is often a record of sequence instead: the first arriver edits the environment, so everything later is judged in a room the incumbent already changed. And the defence may not be run by the incumbent at all, but by whatever attached itself along the way, which is why attacking the thing on its own merits so reliably misses.

So when you catch yourself explaining why the current occupant of a slot is the right one, replace the comparison with a fact: what else was actually available on the day it was chosen, and was any of it evaluated? If you cannot name the alternatives that existed at the time, you are reading an arrival record rather than a selection. That also tells you how to change one, not by arguing merit, which loses to an incumbent that has edited the room, but by creating a genuine vacancy: a rebuild you were doing anyway, a forced re-tender, any moment where the choice must be made again from scratch. Run it this week on the one tool or process you would defend hardest. The same architecture is why a field's standard method is so often the one that arrived when the instrument did, and why the first data pipeline a team builds quietly decides what its models can be taught years later.
