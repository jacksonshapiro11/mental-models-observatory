## ▸ THE UPDATE

**Bitcoin mining lost money before a dollar of overhead inside Core Scientific, while renting the same buildings to AI tenants threw off more gross profit than the whole company earned.**
Self-mining brought in $21.5 million against $33.7 million of cost, a $12.2 million loss at the gross line, in a quarter when revenue doubled. Colocation did $136.7 million of the $164.2 million total, and TeraWulf ran the identical split. This reprices what these companies ever owned, which was an energised connection to the grid, built when bitcoin was the only bidder for stranded power. The fact that resists it is the segment line: secondary coverage puts mining revenue at $27.5 million, likely including hosted capacity, which would shrink the loss. Either way the asset never changed and the tenant did.

**Merck and Moderna's melanoma therapy worked, and the interesting thing about it is that there is no such thing as a dose of it.**
Intismeran autogene with Keytruda met its primary endpoint in 1,137 patients with fully resected melanoma, the first Phase 3 win for a therapy built individually for each patient. Every course is made from that patient's own tumour, encoding up to 34 unique mutations. Every pharmaceutical model since the 1950s rests on spending once and then stamping out identical units at almost no marginal cost. Keytruda is exactly that: $31.7 billion of sales last year on a patent expiring in December 2028. Its replacement has no identical units, so plant throughput binds instead of patent life. The objection that costs most is that biology has industrialised before, and antibodies looked bespoke once too. But automation makes a unit cheaper. It does not make a second one the same.

**The fixed-rate mortgage has a part that was never fixed, and it is pushing FHA borrowers into serious delinquency while the headline number improves.**
The Mortgage Bankers Association put overall delinquency at 4.37 percent in the second quarter, down seven basis points. Seriously delinquent loans, ninety days past due or in foreclosure, rose for a fourth straight quarter: up 227 basis points from a year ago on FHA loans, up six on conventional. What separates them is escrow, the property tax and insurance collected monthly and reset once a year, up 45 percent nationally since 2019 and underwritten at closing. FHA's borrower is the one without reserves. The honest objection is that conventional added only six basis points and conventional is most of the market. But the corner is where the entry-level buyer lives.

**OpenAI has put its largest frontier training run on hold with no restart date, and now spends about a fifth of its monitored inference compute watching its own models.**
Jakub Pachocki and Sam Altman posted the detail on 18 August: training paused on one model, the larger run held indefinitely, monitoring mandatory for every inference involving tools. Twenty percent of serving compute is not a research budget. It is a tax on the thing that earns, and it landed the week the Wall Street Journal reported the quarterly loss widening to $12.3 billion. Which is also the strongest thing said against it: a lab losing that much has a commercial reason to stop the most expensive thing it does. The monitoring resists that reading, because it costs money continuously instead of saving it. We expect the halted run to be the more durable signal, because a compute tax gets optimised down and a lost schedule does not.

**Treasury will buy back at least twice as much long-dated debt per operation from 9 September, paying for it with short-term bills.** It is retiring cheap thirty-year debt and replacing it with borrowing that costs more today, so the long bond rallies while the government's own interest bill goes up immediately.

**Pennsylvania now requires an AI data centre to win local community approval before it is built.** Roughly a billion dollars goes into a hundred-megawatt site before a permit matters, and a queue has a distribution where a vote does not.

**Forty percent of Target's earnings per share was a refund of tariffs the Supreme Court said the government could not charge.** The $994 million credit landed in cost of goods sold, where a windfall reads as merchandising skill.

**Analog Devices guided nine percent above consensus at a gross margin near 74 percent, on the parts between the racks.** Power management and precision timing are qualified over years, not benchmarked, so nobody switches supplier over a score. Boring is where pricing power hides.

**Three Federal Reserve officials dissented for a hike in July, the first three-way hawkish split since 2016.** Hammack, Kashkari and Logan each wanted a quarter point from a committee that held, and every soft number since arrived after they voted.

**Iraq is discounting crude by as much as $29.80 under its own official selling price, with oil revenue halved.** Most of its spending is a payroll it cannot cut, so it keeps selling however weak the price. A seller who cannot stop is what keeps oil prices from rising.

**The average American household expects 7.9 percent inflation next year while the median expects 4.3, and the average decides spending.** The savings rate sits at 2.7 percent, and a minority with no buffer does not behave like the median the models assume.

## ▸ THE TAKE

More than thirty AI models now sit above the European AI Act's compute threshold, which obliges their makers to notify Brussels within two weeks. Call the mechanism verification exposure: the access that produces assurance is the same access that produces intelligence, so the monitored party prices an inspection as a disclosure. A nuclear test ban died in 1963 over three on-site inspections a year against seven, and what got signed excluded the only category that required letting anyone in. We expect AI governance to split along access rather than ambition: declaration-based regimes get agreed quickly, anything requiring standing entry to weights or a facility stalls. The case against us is the Chemical Weapons Convention, which designed bounded access into the instrument and got signed anyway. An honest engineer and an unwilling party produce the identical sentence when they say a scheme cannot be verified.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**The cell pours most of its food on the floor to protect something it needs more.**
Give E. coli all the oxygen and sugar it wants and past a certain growth rate it stops burning sugar all the way down, fermenting it instead and throwing most of the energy away. Markus Basan and colleagues measured why in *Nature*: respiration yields far more energy per molecule but takes twice as much protein to run, and protein is the fixed budget. The cell is careful with apparatus, not careless with food, and which resource to conserve depends on which is scarce.

**The record for atoms ever held in a quantum array is 6,100, and not one of them has run a calculation.** Nobody has bridged the gap to the far smaller count used to run an algorithm. Holding a thing and getting work out of it are separate achievements.

**A sixty-year-old textbook explanation for why insects evolved societies just failed the first proper test anyone ran on it.** Arizona State researchers mapped 69,000 species onto two phylogenies and found nearly the whole signal comes from one clade of wasps and ants. It survived on elegance rather than evidence.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"A beginner should not hold two arrows. It will make him rely on the second arrow and be careless with the first."*

– Yoshida Kenkō, *Essays in Idleness*, c. 1330

You would call the archer prudent, not lazy. He brought a spare because things go wrong. His teacher says the spare is the problem.

The claim is stranger than it looks. It is not that the second arrow makes him fail. It is that holding it changes what his hands do during the first shot, before anything has gone wrong, in a way he cannot feel.

You are holding one right now, and you know which. The second job application open in another tab. The version of the conversation where you do not have to say the hard sentence. The cost is not paid when you fall back on it, but continuously, by the part of you that stays behind to keep it warm.

**Today's practice: find the second arrow in the work you are making right now, the safer version you built in case the real one fails. Remove it before you next sit down.**

## ▸ THE MODEL

### Consciousness & the Hard Problem

In 1693 William Molyneux asked John Locke whether a man born blind, who had learned by touch to tell a cube from a sphere, could tell them apart by sight alone once he could see. Philosophers argued for three centuries. Then a group at MIT tested newly sighted patients days after surgery: near chance at first, right within five.

Some questions are like that: the method was fine and an observation was missing. Others differ in kind. Write the best answer your method can give, in its own vocabulary, and see whether the question still stands. If it does, you are short of vocabulary, not data.

**The decision tool: run that test before you spend.** If it collapses, go get the observation. If it survives, measuring harder will feel like progress and will not be.
