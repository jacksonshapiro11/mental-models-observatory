## Wednesday, August 12, 2026

### The Fed Got Cheaper and Nothing Else Did

*Two prices for the same dollar moved in opposite directions on Tuesday. The market cut September rate-hike odds from roughly two-thirds to under half in a week, and long yields sat at a month-to-date high anyway. Everyone who borrows lives in the second price: renting is cheaper than owning in all fifty of the largest metros, and a company growing revenue 112 percent lost less than its own interest bill. The shooting wars are on a different axis, and they are the exception that keeps the rest honest.*

## ▸ THE UPDATE

**CoreWeave is not losing money selling compute, it is losing money owing for it: its net interest bill for the quarter was $640 million and the loss it reported was $626 million.**
The AI cloud provider reported revenue of $2.58 billion, up 112 percent, and adjusted profit of $1.51 billion at a 59 percent margin. That margin is arithmetically correct and it excludes the only cost that decides solvency. Operating cash was $679 million against $6.4 billion of property and equipment spending, funded by $13.5 billion of new debt. The range is guidance, not a reported figure, so if actual interest came in under its floor, part of the loss is operating and the split does not hold. Backlog reached $104 billion, but a backlog claims future revenue and interest claims present cash. Global Crossing built 101,000 route-miles of fiber against a backlog cited as proof the capacity was pre-sold, and filed for bankruptcy in 2002.

**September rate-hike odds fell from 67 percent to a coin flip in a week and long yields rose anyway, because the long end is pricing government borrowing rather than the Fed.**
Robin Brooks, an economist and no dove, pointed Tuesday at the one developed market where long yields are not rising: Switzerland, whose public debt is a little over 30 percent of output. Kelly Chen supplied a second exemption on opposite machinery: China's deficits do not lift its long yields because capital controls trap the marginal buyer. Two exemptions, one bought with low debt and one with a closed capital account, identify the cause better than any single outlier. Two is also a thin sample, and both differ from the United States on more than debt, so the test may be picking up size and reserve status instead. But core inflation is 2.6 percent now against 3.1 percent when Powell signalled cuts at Jackson Hole last year. Inflation is lower and the long end is higher, which leaves the credibility story explaining the wrong variable.

**The oil market is pricing the odds that one waterway reopens, when five separate parties are currently shooting at commercial ships.**
A third drone in three days hit Libya's Zawiya oil facilities, and the operator warned it will declare force majeure and suspend the refinery if the strikes continue. That makes three separate places taking fire this month rather than one crisis: Hormuz has been shut or conditional since Iran's late-February closure, with the current closure dating from the July collapse of the June 17 memorandum, and Ukrainian drones shut the Caspian Pipeline Consortium's Black Sea terminal three times in July before Kazakh output recovered to 2.08 million barrels a day on August 9. Timothy Ash's tally is the statement no incident report makes: the United States, Iran, Russia, Ukraine and the Houthis are all firing on commercial shipping at once. The five are not independent, and four sit downstream of two conflicts, so a settlement in either could stop three shooters together. Even so, war-risk cover is a percentage of hull value, so each extra 1 percent on a $100 million vessel is another $1 million per voyage, which is where owners start rerouting. The premium reprices on the count of shooters. The barrel reprices on the headline.

**More than ten Southern pulp mills have closed since 2023, and their check is what paid for the thinning that manufactures a sawlog.**
The first thinning at around age fifteen removes small stems only a pulp mill will buy, and that removal concentrates the next twenty years of growth into the trees that become sawtimber. Southeast pine pulpwood averaged about $6.48 a ton on the stump in 2025, on many tracts at or below the cost of cutting and hauling, so a landowner facing a thinning that loses money defers it. University of Georgia researchers modelling Southeastern plantations for the *Journal of Forestry* found land expectation value falls 13 to 34 percent if pulpwood prices halve, and 28 to 68 if pulpwood goes to zero. A deferred thinning is not a write-down, which is also the best case against urgency: an option nobody must exercise comes back if the fibre market clears. What it leaves is denser stands and, two decades out, a thinner supply of large-diameter sawlogs, in a decision nobody books and no reserve carries.

## ▸ THE TAKE

When a dispute is about who counts as a party rather than what the terms are, no better terms can settle it, because every larger offer is a fresh exercise of the authority being contested. Asked how much data-center opposition runs on hostility toward AI, the reporter Jasmine Sun answered "almost none of it," and Stanford's Andrew Hall finds council complaints run on process and transparency. The call: through June 30, 2027, more US states will enact disclosure conditions on data-center siting than construction moratoria. Where it breaks: the contested-project tally describes opposition centred on electricity, water and noise, substantive complaints with prices attached. Money offered under a non-disclosure agreement, to people whose complaint is that the deal was made without them, does not underpay the grievance, it commits it again with a larger number attached.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**Ice Age hunters killed female mammoths, overwhelmingly, and nobody can say why.**
Stockholm's Centre for Palaeogenetics sequenced 521 woolly mammoths for *Current Biology*. At eleven human-associated bone piles dated 20,000 to 30,000 years ago, females were 70 percent of individuals. Where mammoths simply died and were buried, males were 66 percent. The ratio does not lean, it inverts. The obvious explanation, that females were smaller and safer to hunt, does not account for the size of the effect. The authors' other candidate is not preference at all: female-led herds may have moved more predictably, so hunters met them more often.

**Two electronic patterns that were supposed to exclude each other instead share one crystal, and MIT filmed them coming apart and rebuilding.** Cooling erbium tritelluride produced a second wave at right angles to the first, so competing states in a material can be roommates rather than rivals.

**The Cambrian explosion may have been fed, quite literally, by the arrival of animal droppings.** Organic matter used to drift and disperse, while a pellet sinks and delivers it to the seafloor, and bigger animals made bigger pellets, so the waste built the supply chain the diversification ran on.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

You would assume that running a hard conversation through your head in advance is preparation. Mostly it is not. It is attendance.

*"A measure of wisdom each man shall have, but never too much let him know; for the wise man's heart is seldom happy, if wisdom too great he has won."*
– Hávamál, stanza 55, Poetic Edda, trans. Henry Adams Bellows (1923)

You have already had the meeting. You had it at 6:40 this morning in the shower, again in the car, and in the third version you finally said the thing you have been trying to phrase for a week. None of it happened.

The poem calls that state middling-wise and recommends it, not as modesty but as a claim about cost. Foresight and dread run on the same equipment, and the body does not discount for probability. Rehearse four times and you have paid four times for the information from one. The difference is whether anything new arrives on the next run. If it does not, you are not preparing, you are prepaying.

**Today's practice: take the conversation you keep running and draft it once, on paper, in five minutes. Then stop and treat the page as finished.**

## ▸ THE MODEL

### Power Laws & Concentration vs. Diversification

Lewis Fry Richardson spent his last decade sorting every violent conflict between 1820 and 1945 by how many people died, expecting crimes at one end and wars at the other. He found one smooth line, with no natural place to cut it in half.

That is a power law: the average describes no real case, and the largest single observation can rival everything below it. Check whether your biggest recent outcome exceeds the sum of the bottom half. If it does, stop optimizing the median, maximize independent draws, and check they are truly independent, because ten shots that depend on one customer are one shot with ten names. If it does not, there is no jackpot, and the game is the typical result.
