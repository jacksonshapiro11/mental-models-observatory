## ▸ THE UPDATE

**The Market Paid Six Percent for a Deal One Side Says Does Not Exist.**
Trump said talks would begin Monday afternoon and a Hormuz agreement was imminent. Iranian military sources, via Mehr News on Sunday, called it a lie and an attempt to blackmail the Gulf's rulers, and Iran's foreign ministry said nothing was scheduled. The same morning, UKMTO logged an explosion near a tanker 20 nautical miles northeast of Khasab. Crude fell almost 6 percent anyway. The second explanation offered does not survive arithmetic either: the OPEC+ adjustment is 188 thousand barrels a day, roughly 0.18 percent of global supply, and it does not take effect until September. That cannot move oil six percent. What moved it was the claim, and the claim has one signatory. The nearest hard date is August 16, when the June 17 memorandum between Washington and Tehran, a 60-day instrument, lapses with nothing drafted to replace it. Watch the price on August 17. If crude still carries today's discount after the only document either side actually signed has expired, the market has priced a peace straight through its own paperwork.

**The Committee Has Stopped Arguing About Cuts and Started Arguing About Hikes.**
John Williams, the New York Fed president and the FOMC's vice chair, told Reuters in an interview published at 6:21 Monday morning that if inflation is not on a path back to 2 percent, "it would absolutely be appropriate to act to get us on a trajectory that does." Act, in that sentence, means raise. The Committee held at 3.50 to 3.75 percent on July 29 with three dissents, and all three dissenters wanted to hike. June PCE ran 3.7 percent, and Williams' own horizon for a sustained return to target is 2028. Every model in the market still prices the question as when cuts arrive. The distribution inside the room runs from hold to hike, with nothing on the other side. Watch the August and September core PCE prints, which Williams named as his decision input.

**Two Companies Marked Their Own Homework and Set the Number for the Whole Index.**
FactSet's John Butters published the blended figure on July 31: S&P 500 earnings grew 47.4 percent last quarter, the fastest since 2021. Take out Alphabet and Amazon and it falls to 28.8 percent, while the aggregate surprise collapses from 31.4 percent to 9.2. What those two contributed was not revenue. Alphabet's GAAP earnings of $9.11 a share against a $2.88 estimate carried a $98 billion gain, and Amazon's $5.75 against $1.82 carried $53.4 billion of non-operating income it attributes largely to a stake in Anthropic that Amazon itself funded. Both figures are true, and true about different economies: one that sells things, one that revalues what it owns. The second is what the New York Fed cited Monday as its reason not to worry about leverage in the AI complex. Watch FactSet's re-blend on Friday August 7, the first date this gap either narrows or hardens.

**Someone Reproduced Half of the Big AI Math Result in a Day, With No Special Equipment.**
Zvi Mowshowitz reported Monday that the mathematician Levent Alpoge pointed a Claude model at five of the ten problems OpenAI's Astra system had just solved, and had independent solutions inside 24 hours with no special harness. His term for it is a proofs overhang: once you know which problems are reachable, the results turn out to have been sitting there, unasked. Gary Marcus supplied the uncomfortable version, which is that OpenAI ran no control group. The cost ladder points the same direction. The $2,000 figure covers all ten proofs combined rather than each, so a marginal Astra problem ran near $200, and a DeepMind team solved nine of 353 open Erdős problems at a few hundred each: two labs, two architectures, one order of magnitude. Once an attempt is that cheap, the scarce input stops being compute or capability and becomes knowing where to point it. Thomas Bloom, who maintains the Erdős database, named the real bottleneck: nobody has read the 200-page proofs, and nobody is going to.

**The Market Will Not Even Pay the Guaranteed Part of This $8 Billion Deal.**
Curium is buying Lantheus for up to $8 billion. The certain money is $102.50 a share. The other $12 is a contingent value right paying only if sales targets are hit by 2030, the part the buyer declined to underwrite. The release quotes premiums up to 38 percent, measured to May 21, the last day before the deal leaked and ten weeks before anyone signed. Against Friday's actual $99.64 close, the certain cash was a 2.9 percent premium. Monday's close came in at $101.19, $1.31 below the certain cash with payment nine months out. Charge that cash leg an ordinary bill rate and the $12 right is left worth about $1.60. There is precedent for zero: Bristol-Myers carried its $9 Celgene right at $6.4 billion, liso-cel missed its December 2020 deadline by five weeks, and holders got nothing. A headline price is what a banker will print. The certain money is what a buyer will sign, and the gap between them is the seller's own estimate of its case.

**One Balance Sheet Now Holds One Ether in Twenty, and It Is Not the Bitcoin Trade.**
BitMine disclosed Monday that as of Sunday afternoon it held 5,797,813 ether, alongside 209 bitcoin and $173 million in cash and securities, for $11.3 billion in total. Against a supply of 120.7 million coins that stack is 4.80 percent, a figure the company states and which recomputes exactly. The number worth sitting with is not the eleven billion. It is that one company has withdrawn roughly one ether in twenty from circulation. The instrument underneath is also different from the bitcoin version everyone benchmarks it against. Bitcoin pays nothing, so a bitcoin treasury survives only by issuing stock above net asset value. Ether can be staked, so the same withdrawn float earns a yield and the carry can run negative rather than positive. Strategy proved the model works when the asset only goes up. Nobody has run it through a flat year on an asset that pays.

**The Pentagon Stopped Buying Drones and Started Lending Money to Whoever Makes the Parts.**
The Department of War's Office of Strategic Capital committed up to $820 million to PDW Holdings on July 31, and the release is specific about what the money buys: not airframes, but domestic manufacturing of propulsion, power and control, and vision systems for small drones. Director David Lorch's line is the one to keep: PDW will be one of several domestic component producers receiving these loan commitments under Executive Order 14307. The state is subsidizing a layer rather than a product, and a cost curve pushed down at propulsion, power and optics arrives as margin for whoever builds those and as pressure on the primes buying them. Two cautions the coverage dropped: this is a conditional commitment rather than a disbursement, and Lux Capital, which backs PDW, is amplifying it. The release publishes no aggregate program size, so "several" has no denominator, which makes this a direction you can read and not yet a position you can size.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**Silk Was in Central Asia Seventeen Centuries Before the Road Named After It.**
A team led by Xinyi Liu at Washington University in St. Louis reported in Science Advances in late July that three silkworm cocoons excavated at Sapalli Tepe in Uzbekistan are Bombyx mori, the domesticated silkworm, and date directly to between 1940 and 1765 BCE. Charcoal from mulberry trees lay beside them, and mulberry leaves are the only thing Bombyx mori eats. The Silk Road is conventionally dated to the second century BCE. Routes get named for whatever finally moves at scale, which is usually the last thing to arrive rather than the first.

**Which Sibling You Are Tracks Your Risk of 150 Separate Diseases.**
Benjamin Kramer and colleagues at the University of Chicago pulled commercial insurance records covering 5.1 million two-child families, 10.3 million people, and tested 418 conditions. Roughly a third showed significant birth-order associations after correcting for multiple comparisons, with firstborns carrying more allergy, autism and migraine diagnoses. The proposed mechanism is the hygiene hypothesis, that a firstborn's immune system meets fewer other children early. It is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed, which is the honest caveat. The finding is not the effect size. It is that a variable this cheap to collect sat unexamined at this scale until somebody bothered.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"There is nothing so beautiful and legitimate as to play the man well and properly, and no knowledge so arduous as to know how to live this life well and naturally."*
– Michel de Montaigne, Of Experience

Montaigne wrote that at the end of an essay largely about kidney stones, his digestion, and the poverty of his own memory. He had spent twenty years reading the ancients on how to die well and concluded, against himself, that the harder subject was the one nobody had assigned him: how to have an ordinary Tuesday and be inside it.

You would assume living well is the competence that rewards effort most visibly. It is the only one that leaves nothing behind, and that is exactly why he calls it arduous. Every other competence produces evidence. Work produces output, fitness produces a number, even grief produces a story you can tell. An hour spent well produces nothing you can show anyone, so the only way to defend it is to have decided in advance that it needed no defending. Most people never make that decision, so they smuggle a second purpose into everything: the walk taken with headphones so that it also counts as podcast time, the weekend that has to produce a story by Monday or it did not happen.

That instinct is not laziness or vanity. It is a solvency habit applied where solvency is not the question. You make the hour pay because you learned, correctly, that hours which do not pay are how people fall behind. The error is only in the scope.

**Today's practice:** Make one thing today with no downstream use. Write the page you will not publish, cook the meal nobody is coming for, play badly for twenty minutes with the door shut. Two rules: finish it, and show it to nobody. Then watch how fast you start looking for somewhere to put it. That reflex is the arduousness Montaigne named.

## ▸ THE MODEL

### Fractals & Self-Similarity Across Scales

Lewis Fry Richardson, a Quaker meteorologist studying why nations go to war, guessed that risk scales with the border two neighbours share. So he went looking for border lengths. Spain reported its frontier with Portugal at 987 kilometres. Portugal reported the same frontier at 1,214. Neither was lying. Measure a border with a fifty-kilometre yardstick and you cut every corner; with a ten-kilometre one you follow the bends the long stick skipped, and the total grows. It never converges: the shape looks the same at every magnification. So before planning on any number, ask what it would be at half the resolution and at twice it. If the answers cluster you have a quantity. If they spread you have a measurement procedure, and the only portable thing is how fast the answer moves as you look closer.
