### Nine Basis Points on Nothing

*Four of today's stories turn on something that has not happened yet, and the not-happening is free to whoever is not doing it. A bond buyback that has spent no money moved the long end, a chip guide that assumes no China data center compute revenue is the number the market trades tonight, and a postponed vote stopped shipyards ordering low-carbon vessels. Commitments cost nothing to hold and move prices anyway. The exception is that same long end, which moved with Germany, Britain and France on a day when the only news was American.*

## ▸ THE UPDATE

**A Treasury buyback that has not spent a dollar took nine basis points off the thirty-year, and then four countries' long rates moved together on purely American news.**
It does not begin until 9 September, and the whole increment is fourteen billion dollars across eight weeks: an unexercised backstop that costs nothing while it stays credible. Every nominal bucket out to ten years already carried a four billion dollar cap while the two longest carried two, so doubling the long end only restores parity. Then look at the same session elsewhere: the German, British and French long ends eased with the American one, six to nine basis points, on a day whose only news was American. That is evidence against our own story. If an American fiscal decision were setting the price of long money, Berlin and Paris would not have moved on it, which leaves two possibilities: either an announcement that has spent nothing is setting the world's long rate, or the long rate was never America's to set. Either way, a portfolio hedged only against American fiscal risk is covering a fraction of what it owns.

**Nvidia's own guidance for tonight's results assumes zero China data center compute revenue, so the number everyone will read as a verdict on AI demand is a policy assumption wearing a forecast's clothes.**
It guided to roughly 91 billion dollars at a 75 percent gross margin on that assumption, and analysts sit nine tenths of a percent above the midpoint, which endorses the guide and nothing more. With the disputed market removed from the forecast, a beat and a miss now measure the same narrower thing. The fact that resists this read is the company's own behaviour: it has been running a shortage it could clear by raising prices and has not, which the analyst Byrne Hobart reads as rationing rather than scarcity. We expect tonight's print to be received as a verdict on demand and to be, by its own construction, a verdict on policy.

**An American fuel subsidy fenced to North America now helps set the price of soap and dog food, and the herd it draws on is the smallest since 1951.**
Beef fat is not produced. It is left over. Renderers collect it from slaughterhouses, so the volume is set by how many cattle were killed, and for a century the buyers were soapmakers, oleochemical plants and pet-food formulators. Then Section 45Z, the credit that pays refiners for low-carbon fuel, got a fence: from this year the feedstock must be grown in the United States, Mexico or Canada. Demand a cargo from anywhere used to satisfy now points at the byproduct of a herd of 86.2 million head. The objection is that a shrinking herd explains the price with no policy in the story, but it does not explain who pays. A subsidy fenced by origin turns an elastic global supply curve into an inelastic local one, and the bill lands on whoever was already buying that input for something unrelated.

**Iran has stopped sending its own officers to attack exiles abroad and started hiring criminals, because a disposable contractor is cheaper than a burned intelligence officer.**
The New Yorker reported on Monday that the regime is recruiting Russian mobsters, Hells Angels bikers and Swedish teenagers to attack Iranians living in exile. Phillip Smyth, who tracks Iran's proxy networks, put the appeal plainly: criminals "can obtain false documents, cross borders, rent safe houses, move money, and disappear." A regime whose currency has more than halved again in eighteen months cannot afford officers abroad, and a criminal is paid in the target country's money. The last word in Smyth's list is the objection: disappearing is deniability rather than thrift, and that is worth buying at any exchange rate. Either way, Iranian coercion becomes a line in European policing budgets that no government attributes to Tehran, paid by the exiles in a currency nobody can retaliate against.

## ▸ THE TAKE

Both halves of the American housing market reported July and disagreed: existing homes up 2.0 percent on the year to a median 434,100 dollars, new homes down 0.9 to 393,800. The half cutting price carries 9.6 months of unsold stock and the half raising it carries 4.6, which is backwards until you notice that only one of these sellers may refuse to sell: a homeowner who declines to list keeps his number, while a builder holds a finished house and a construction loan. Call it the refusal margin, because a blocked repricing reroutes in full to the margin the decider does not control, so whoever can walk away pays in volume and whoever cannot pays in price. We expect the two medians to keep printing opposite signs into next spring, and the read fails if distress arrives in size, because an owner who must move cannot refuse. Before believing any price, ask who in that market is allowed to.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**The worst thing that ever happened to it is what slowed the crack down.**
A crack in a loaded metal part creeps forward a little on every cycle. Put one unusually large load through it and the crack lurches ahead, then grows more slowly than before for thousands of cycles, sometimes stalling altogether. Wolf Elber published the reason in 1970: the overload stretches the metal at the crack tip past the point of return, and the material around it squeezes that zone shut when the load comes off, so part of every later cycle does no damage. Every engineering code adds the damage up and cannot see this. Hours worked, incidents logged, changes shipped: a sum is precisely the operation that destroys order.

**People who live past 110 carry roughly four times the proportion of a rare cancer-killing immune cell that younger adults do.** Researchers at the University of Osaka put the median at 4 percent in younger people and 17.6 in supercentenarians, which makes extreme longevity look less like avoiding damage than like never calling off the search for it.

**Fungi and bacteria carried by astronauts could survive for weeks to months in the permanently shadowed craters at the Moon's south pole, NASA modelling finds.** Ordinary black mould was the most resilient of the five organisms simulated, and the cold and dark that preserve ancient lunar chemistry preserve what we bring, so the control sample is contaminated before anyone runs the experiment.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"All relationships of people to each other rest, as a matter of course, upon the precondition that they know something about each other."*

– Georg Simmel, "The Sociology of Secrecy and of Secret Societies," *American Journal of Sociology* (1906)

Simmel opens with that sentence and then spends the essay taking it apart. The precondition is not that you know a lot about someone. It is that you know a particular amount, and the amount differs in every relationship you are in. The merchant needs to know whether you can pay and nothing else; the teacher needs to know what you have understood and not what you are afraid of. Each pairing has its own ratio of knowledge to ignorance, and the part nobody quotes is his claim about it: the ratio is not a limit on the relationship, it is the relationship's shape.

Which is why the colleague who tells you about his marriage over lunch has not become your friend. Run it the other way, though, because that one is probably yours: a friendship fed with disclosure for years, and which has not moved, reached its ratio long ago.

**Today's practice: pick the relationship you have been feeding with information and which has not moved. Tell that person nothing new about yourself this week. Ask them instead for something small and specific: a favour, an introduction, an opinion they will have to defend. A relationship changes when something is asked of it, not when something is told to it.**
