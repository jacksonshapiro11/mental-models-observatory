## The Wild Card

- **The FDA authorised a robot that draws blood, and the condition it attached is a supervision ratio.** On 27 August the agency granted De Novo authorisation to Aletta, made by the Dutch firm Vitestro, creating a new device classification for autonomous robotic phlebotomy. The machine uses near-infrared light and Doppler ultrasound to find a vein and tell it from an artery, then applies the tourniquet, preps the skin, inserts and disposes of the needle, swaps the tubes and puts on the bandage. - **The largest study of personality genetics ever run found that its answers hold whether you rated yourself or somebody who knows you did.** A meta-analysis published in *Nature* on 2 September pooled 46 cohorts, between 611,037 and 1.14 million participants depending on the trait, and identified 1,260 lead genetic variants associated with the Big Five traits, 824 of them new. A five-question personality inventory is the kind of soft instrument serious people discount on sight, and this one turns out to be stable enough that its signal survives changing the narrator.

## The Signal

**Context signal: The fight over your electricity bill is not about how much power a data centre uses. It is about a rule that decides who pays for the wires.**

That data centres are raising power prices is the most-covered utility story in America. US residential electricity has climbed roughly forty percent since 2021, to about 17.29 cents a kilowatt-hour last year and a projected 18.02 this one. What is actually being decided is narrower and almost unwatched: a cost-allocation rule.

Connect a very large load and someone pays for the network upgrades it triggers. A ratepayer-advocate complaint at the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission counts 130 large-load interconnection projects in PJM, of which six required the requesting customer to pay its own connection costs. The rest, more than four billion dollars a year of upgrades on that filing's advocacy arithmetic, is spread across everyone in the zone. On 18 June 2026 FERC ordered all six grid operators to show cause why their large-load tariffs remain just and reasonable, or to file changes. In March the largest developers signed a voluntary Ratepayer Protection Pledge promising to pay for their own upgrades. A pledge is not a tariff, and it exists precisely because the tariff does not.

Two days ago this brief showed data-centre load adding $6.5 billion to a PJM capacity auction, $6.2 billion of it for load that was forecast rather than operating. This is the other cheque, and it is for the megawatts that actually get built. Rutgers' New Jersey State Policy Lab concluded this year that data centres are mostly not yet raising household bills, and Marketplace found territories in July where they lowered them. **A socialised upgrade is one no one must fund up front. Assigning it to the load that triggered it does not shrink it. It only moves the cheque forward, from a rate case to a shovel.**

We expect the rewrite to arrive through 2027 as a longer interconnection queue rather than a cheaper household bill, because capital a developer must approve moves slower than capital it can socialise. That lands on whoever signs the interconnection agreement, Microsoft (MSFT), Meta (META), Oracle (ORCL) and the colocation layer, Equinix (EQIX) and Digital Realty (DLR). The transmission owners are paid either way, earning a regulated return on the upgrade whoever writes the cheque: Dominion (D), Exelon (EXC), FirstEnergy (FE). The household is the only party whose outcome is genuinely uncertain, which is odd, given it is the one the argument is named after.

**When a shop sells an advertisement to the brand whose products it also buys, that dollar has two legal homes, and each side gets to choose the flattering one**

Retail media, the sponsored slot beside the search result, is the most profitable line most retailers own, and on 29 June this brief told you to buy it. This is the objection to our own call, and it is not about demand.

A payment from a brand to a retailer that also stocks it is governed twice. Under ASC 705-20 the retailer books money from a supplier as a reduction of the cost of goods bought from that supplier, unless the supplier receives a distinct service, in which case it is revenue, capped at that service's standalone selling price. Under ASC 606 the brand books the same payment as a reduction of its own revenue, unless it receives a distinct service, in which case it is an expense. Same dollar, two filers, two judgments, no reconciliation between them.

Deloitte published dedicated guidance on this in **October 2022**, and the vintage is the argument: the framework everyone applies predates nearly all of the scaling it governs. No company here is accused of misclassifying anything. The finding is that the disclosure does not exist to tell you, because no major retailer publishes its standalone-selling-price basis, or the split between distinct-service ad revenue and vendor funding credited against cost of sales.

**A payment two accounting frameworks can each classify favourably will be classified favourably twice, and an industry's reported margin can rise without a single price changing.**

We expect the first mark to be a disclosure change rather than a restatement, in the FY2026 annual reports filed early in 2027. Nobody's cash changes. What shrinks is the reported advertising revenue the market is putting a multiple on. The exposure sits where the ad multiple is highest against the least independently observable inventory: Walmart (WMT), Kroger (KR) and, most acutely, Instacart (CART), whose advertisers are also its suppliers. Amazon (AMZN) is least exposed, because a real third-party marketplace supplies the observable price the others must estimate. Procter & Gamble (PG) and Kraft Heinz (KHC) hold the other end of the same judgment, and the day one of them reclassifies is the day the question becomes everyone's.

# ▸ THE TAKE

### You Can Only Count Who Stayed

The renminbi's share of global payments peaked at 4.7 percent in July 2024 and was 2.88 percent on July 2026 data, sixth place, behind the Canadian dollar. Every de-dollarization argument published this year rests on that number, and the number is produced by SWIFT, which counts the payments that travel over SWIFT.

Call the mechanism *the gauge is the gate*. American power over a transaction runs on two legs that can come apart. The first is denomination: a dollar payment settles through an American correspondent bank, which is why a foreign institution fears losing dollar clearing more than any fine, and it is why a secondary sanction deters at all. The second is the message, the instruction naming who paid whom, without which a designation is decoration. SWIFT carries most of that traffic, and since 1 August 2010 it has also been a channel the US Treasury can compel, under the EU-US Terrorist Finance Tracking Program agreement. So the scoreboard is a share of the second leg, computed by the second leg's operator, over the second leg's own traffic. China's alternative exits both at once. CIPS is a renminbi clearing system with its own messaging standard, run out of Shanghai, and a payment that moves there leaves the enforcer's field of view and leaves the number meant to be scoring it in the same instant, in the direction that makes the dollar look stronger.

The reflex is to say the statistic is lying. It is not, which is the more interesting problem. Martin Chorzempa's study for the Peterson Institute this week runs the honest test from outside the system: Hong Kong's CHATS, which clears roughly three times CIPS's payment volume, saw average daily renminbi settlement fall by more than a trillion yuan between June 2024 and June 2026, while CIPS gained only about two hundred billion, a fifth of the offset. The retreat is real. The metric is accurate. It is accurate about a question nobody is asking. What it cannot see grew over the same window: CIPS went from 79 direct participants in January 2023 to 210 today. Data that goes missing *because of its own value* is what Donald Rubin's 1976 taxonomy calls missing not at random, and its defining property is that the bias cannot be bounded from inside the observed data. More SWIFT data can never repair a SWIFT-share statistic.

We expect the two series to keep moving apart, and the commentary to keep quoting the one that publishes monthly. Through September 2027, SWIFT's monthly renminbi share does not close above 4 percent, and CIPS direct participants pass 260. Both halves are checkable by anyone: SWIFT publishes the tracker, CIPS publishes its participant list.

Now the case against. The strongest objection is that the two legs have not actually come apart. Chorzempa's own count gives CIPS 210 direct participants against 1,619 indirect ones, and an indirect participant reaches CIPS through a direct one, so a great many of those legs are still carried on SWIFT messaging. If most CIPS volume still generates a SWIFT message somewhere in the chain, the gauge and the gate are one instrument in a redrawn diagram and nothing has moved. That fraction is not published, and it is not available to anyone outside Beijing, which is uncomfortably close to the hole this argument has just accused the consensus of standing in.

There is also a decade of evidence against arguments of this shape. The renminbi's SWIFT share reached 2.8 percent in late 2015 and sat near 2 percent for the five years after, while China's goods trade settled in renminbi went from 23 percent in 2015 to 11 percent in 2017. CIPS was founded in that same year, and the quiet alternative architecture was nominated then too. That case has been wrong for ten years, because a system nobody is compelled to use is a monument rather than a blindfold.

Chorzempa's own conclusion is the third objection: CIPS is an insurance policy, dormant capacity built to be activated only if sanctions force China off the dollar. On that reading American visibility is unimpaired today, the erosion is zero right up until the day it is total, and nothing in the present numbers should change anyone's behaviour. The test that separates his reading from this one is publishable: were CIPS, the BIS or the CPMI to establish that a majority of CIPS cross-border legs still generate a SWIFT message, the separation claim fails and the share statistic is a sound proxy after all.

What survives either reading is the question worth carrying elsewhere. Of any number used to police something, ask whether the thing that produces the number is the same thing that produces the power. Tax compliance rates, platform moderation statistics, disease surveillance, audited emissions: wherever the measuring apparatus and the enforcing apparatus are one apparatus, everyone who leaves improves the number and reduces the reach at the same moment, and the report reads best on the morning there is least left to report. The Take of 1 September argued that a feared sovereign flow had already happened in plain sight. This is its other half: the flow worth watching is the one that stopped being counted.

# ▸ INNER GAME

Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela, working in Santiago in the early 1970s, asked what makes something alive rather than merely complicated. Their answer was not metabolism, and it was not reproduction. A living thing, they argued, is a system whose only product is itself: it continuously manufactures the components that manufacture it, and the boundary between it and the world is something it is making right now rather than something it has. Stop the production and there is nothing left underneath. There was never anything underneath.

Take that seriously about a person and a comfortable idea goes.

You have a set of sentences you use about yourself. Patient. Not a details person. Someone who follows through. You hear them as descriptions of a thing that exists, which is why the days you do not act like them feel like a betrayal. Of what, exactly, is never asked. There is nothing stored to betray. There is only what is being produced, and a sentence with nothing behind it this month is not a self you are failing to live up to. It is a factory that stopped and left the sign up.

Which is the argument with Adam Phillips from three days ago. He said the unlived life wins every argument because it is vague. This is the sharper reason it wins: it is the one version of you that is not being manufactured, so it never shows wear. The real one is rebuilt daily and looks it.

**Today's practice: write down the three sentences you use to describe yourself, and beside each one put the date you last did the thing it claims. Then add the sentence you would never have put on the list, the thing you actually did most this month. That one is not a failure to be who you are. It is the only entry with a date on it.**

# ▸ THE MODEL

### Social Reality Construction & Collective Belief

On 18 November 1883 the United States had two noons. Until that morning, noon in any town was the moment the sun stood highest over it, so Boston's noon and Washington's noon sat about twenty-four minutes apart. The railroads could not run a timetable on that. They declared four standard time zones and started using them. No legislature had voted, and the federal government did not ratify the arrangement until 1918. For thirty-five years the legal time in most of America was solar time and the time everybody actually used was a private company's invention.

Notice what made railroad time real, because it was not agreement. Solar noon was the accurate one and everybody knew it. What made railroad time real is that it acquired an enforcement layer within months: printed timetables, scheduled departures, wages docked for lateness, a train that left without you. The clock did not win an argument. It acquired a penalty.

That is the mechanism, and it generalises. A social fact begins as enough people acting as though something is so, and it becomes load-bearing at the moment an institution attaches a cost to acting otherwise. Belief produces behaviour, behaviour produces infrastructure, and the infrastructure is what does the holding. Whether the fact is really real stops being the question. It has consequences, and consequences are what real means in practice.

A different domain, same shape. When a new diagnostic category enters a medical manual, the philosopher Ian Hacking observed, it does not merely label people who were already there. It changes them. Insurers pay for it, clinicians look for it, patients describe themselves through it, and researchers then study a population the category helped assemble. Hacking called this a looping effect. The category and the people it names move toward each other until the category fits, which is not evidence that it fit to begin with.

The sizing question runs both directions. Too little of this model and you treat conventions as laws of nature, arguing about whether a border or a credential is deserved when the only live question is who enforces it. Too much and you decide every constraint is negotiable, which is how someone ends up insisting the sun should be their noon. The test is not whether people believe it. It is whether anything would still charge you for ignoring it. Gravity charges you if nobody believes in it. A time zone stops charging you the moment nobody does.

The failure mode is where most effort goes to die. Believing something is socially constructed tempts you to think persuasion can change it, and persuasion almost never can, because the thing is not held by argument. It is held by coordination. Convince one person the railroad clock is arbitrary and you have not freed them, you have made them late. Nothing moves until enough people move at once that the first mover stops paying a penalty, which is why such facts sit for decades and then go over in a season.

So the tool is one question, and it is not the one people ask. When you want to change a belief a group holds, do not ask whether it is true. Ask who else would have to change their mind at the same moment for the first person to be better off moving, and write the number down. One or two, and you have a conversation to have. Thousands, and you do not have an argument problem. You have a coordination problem, and the lever is never the evidence.

# ▸ DISCOVERY

### The Spare Copy Is Not Insurance. It Is a Lottery Ticket With an Expiry Date.

Once in a great while an organism reproduces and the offspring carries the entire genome twice. Whole-genome duplication is the largest single-generation change biology permits, and it is usually lethal or sterilising, since human polyploid embryos abort early. The survivors are the interesting part, because a doubled genome hands an organism a bulk stock of redundant genes, and one copy can keep doing the old job while the other mutates freely into something new. That is the textbook account. The recent work is about what happens next. A November 2025 paper in *Genome Biology and Evolution* examined the New Zealand mud snail *Potamopyrgus antipodarum*, whose Lake Alexandrina populations carry three or four chromosome sets rather than the standard two. The duplication is under a million years old. Roughly sixty percent of the doubled genes are already gone. And Sarah Otto's yeast, handed the antifungal nystatin, adapts to it *worse* when it is carrying the extra copies than when it is not.

The intuition this breaks is that redundancy is insurance. Insurance is held, unchanged, against a named failure, and the premium buys you the option of doing nothing. This is not that. The redundancy is spent, pruned at speed, most of it deleted, and the surviving value sits entirely in the small fraction that acquired a *different* job. The yeast result is worse for the insurance reading than the deletions are, because the duplicate can degrade performance against the specific stressor in front of you. Carrying two of everything is expensive, the expense is paid continuously, and the payoff arrives only if the environment changes hard enough that the current optimised configuration stops working. So a spare copy is not a hedge against the shock you can name. It is raw material for a job that does not exist yet, and it has a decay rate.

So this week, take one duplicated thing you carry, a second supplier, an overlapping process, two people covering the same ground, a system nobody has switched off, and put it in one of exactly two boxes. Insurance: write the named failure it protects against and the trigger that calls on it. Raw material: write the date by which it must acquire a distinct job of its own. Anything fitting neither box is not slack, it is the sixty percent, and it is already being deleted by everyone's small decisions whether or not you make a large one. The check is one week away and costs nothing: you can point at the sentence you wrote, or you cannot. The same architecture is why a research group keeping a slower, older method alive is not hedging against the fast one breaking. It is holding the only thing that can become the next method.
