### Bessent Left the Oil Alone

*Washington's largest ever Iran sanctions package landed on Monday without oil in it, and without the Chinese banks paying for most of Iran's exports, because crude raises the long end of the curve Treasury is trying to hold down. That omission set the day's shape: what bound outcomes was usually a rule written earlier rather than a price, a 1930 tariff statute or a 2016 navigation spec. The exception is Iranian oil, where the squeeze is a shortage of ships.*

## ▸ THE UPDATE

**Washington's biggest ever sanctions package left out the one authority that would have bitten, Iran's oil, because the instrument that hurts Iran most costs America most.**
Operation Economic Outcast designated close to sixty entities on Monday and added five secondary-sanction authorities over digital assets, technology, gold, aviation and shipping. Petroleum powers already existed and were not expanded, and the large Chinese banks financing the trade were left alone, in a market where Beijing buys over 80 percent of Iran's seaborne oil. The strongest objection is Bessent's own: he expects a major financial institution designated by Friday, so this may be a sequence, not a carve-out. Until a large Chinese bank appears there, the omissions tell you what Washington cannot afford.

**Treasury is buying back thirty-year bonds while a new law forces stablecoins into ninety-three-day bills, which is the government shortening its own debt without the Fed.**
Under the GENIUS Act every compliant stablecoin dollar must sit against Treasuries of ninety-three days or less, and Treasury's 17 August rule extends that abroad. Estimates of the extra bill demand run from several hundred billion dollars to more than $2 trillion by 2030. We expect the front end to be owned increasingly by holders a regulation put there. The clause that may let a foreign bank's US branch back a coin with a balance at the Fed is our reading, not the statute's. Still, an issuer that must hold bills is a different buyer from one that wanted to.

**Putting an autonomous pilot on a new aircraft has gone from three years to under two weeks, and the man whose company did it says the brake is now the buyer.**
Brandon Tseng, who co-founded Shield AI, tracked the curve in War on the Rocks: three years on an F-16, then two engineers and under two weeks on a Ukrainian attack drone, with 90 percent of the code unchanged. When nine tenths moves free to the next airframe, autonomy becomes a layer bought once rather than a project priced per platform. Tseng's own forecast moved the other way, from autonomous defense everywhere by 2035 to 2040 or 2045, blaming procurement rather than technology. A supplier who must be ten times better before the Pentagon will buy is describing a customer with no line item.

**A meaningful part of this year's improving cash flow at research-heavy American companies is a one-time tax catch-up, and it stops in 2027.**
The 2017 tax law made companies spread domestic research costs over five years from 2022, quietly raising their cash taxes. Section 174A of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act reversed that, letting them deduct the whole stockpiled 2022 to 2024 balance across 2025 and 2026. That catch-up ends, so 2027 shows a step down the operating businesses did not cause. Where this could be wrong is the reaction, not the arithmetic: a company that flags the drop on an earnings call turns a surprise into a known adjustment. What no filing shows is that 174A covers domestic research only, so where a company's engineers sit decides how big its drop is.

## ▸ THE TAKE

A factor model finds shared causes by looking at what moves together in the same year, so a cause arriving on a different date everywhere gets filed as local. Fernández-Villaverde and Norrick fit one factor to 236 countries: the common component peaked in 1978, and 219 country trends are negative with not one leveling off. That matters because almost every thirty-year projection you own assumes fertility drifts back to replacement, and we expect China's next birth count to fall again from 7.92 million. Korea is the live objection, printing 0.80 for 2025 and a second consecutive rise, and a period rate is depressed whenever couples postpone. Call it the staggered cause: when independent processes agree on direction 219 times and differ only on timing, they were never independent.

## ▸ INTERESTING THINGS

**Soil sterilised with gamma radiation kept breathing for six years after everything alive inside it was dead.**
Sébastien Fontaine, a French soil biochemist, sterilised soil in sealed jars and waited for the carbon dioxide to stop, because soil that emits it has microbes in it. It did not stop. Fifteen years later the same jars, measured at 2,442 days, were still at it. Carbon dioxide was the assay, the accepted stand-in for there is life in here, and it read positive six years after the life was gone. So pick the number you would defend hardest as proof something works, and name the second thing that could move it.

**As much as nine in ten synapses in your visual cortex carry predictions downward rather than sensation upward.** Lisa Feldman Barrett and Earl Miller argue in Nature Reviews Neuroscience that seeing is mostly the brain checking a guess it already committed to.

**An American submarine sank a ship with a torpedo in March, the first time since the Second World War.** USS Charlotte hit the Iranian frigate IRIS Dena, and Thomas Shugart's point is that eight decades separate that torpedo from the one before, so combat experience can be an inheritance rather than a holding.

## ▸ THE MEDITATION

*"All violent feelings have the same effect. They produce in us a falseness in all our impressions of external things, which I would generally characterize as the 'pathetic fallacy.'"*

– John Ruskin, *Modern Painters*, Volume III (1856)

Ruskin was complaining about poets who call the foam on a wave cruel, and his objection was technical: the foam is not cruel, the writer is upset, and the adjective reports the writer rather than the sea. It describes what your mind does to a thing before you have looked at it.

Apply that to your own calendar tonight. Take what you have been avoiding and strip every adjective off it. Not the awful conversation you keep putting off with your landlord, but a ten-minute call in which you each say a number.

Dread is made almost entirely of adjectives. The event underneath is usually small and boring, and you cannot see it because you have been carrying a version written to justify not doing it.

**Today's practice: pick the one thing you have moved forward three times. Write a single sentence describing what physically happens during it, with no adjectives and no words about how it will feel. Then do it, or put it on the calendar at a named hour.**

## ▸ THE MODEL

### Compounding Human Connections

For fifty years, on the Monday nearest each full moon, a dozen men rode to a house outside Birmingham for dinner: a button manufacturer, a potter, a doctor, an instrument maker, a minister interested in gases. Out of those dinners came the separate condenser, the discovery of oxygen, and industrial pottery. They convened to eat.

**The value of a connection is almost never the connection itself. It is the connections it makes reachable.** The button manufacturer did not hand the instrument maker a better idea. He handed him a doctor, who handed him a minister with a gas shed.

So before spending an hour of social time, ask whether this person makes others reachable to you, and whether they could describe you accurately to a stranger unasked. Two noes is contact, and contact does not compound.
