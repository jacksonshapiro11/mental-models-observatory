## The shape of the residual error is the important part

All three remaining disagreements run the same way: **the cold readers passed units the owner
failed.** Zero cells where the readers failed something the owner passed and prior exposure cannot
explain it.

**The Reader's residual error is leniency, not false alarm.** For a system that actuates on failure
that is the safe direction — it produces misses, not spurious rewrites of good prose. It also means
the unanimity-to-actuate rule for nights 1–7 costs less than feared: the instrument was already
erring toward silence.

**And it makes the case for delta 2 mechanically.** Two of the three owner-harsh cells are Capuchins,
where his note is literally *"what is the learning?"* — a so-what question, on a unit six of six cold
readers stated back correctly. The Reader was not wrong about the claim. It was blind to the axis the
owner was judging on.

# 5. PER-ELEMENT — NOT SCOREABLE, AND THAT IS THE FINDING

Amendment 7 asks for ≥80% per-element agreement. **It cannot be computed. The owner supplied five
usable element tags in 34 units.**

| what he used | count |
|---|---:|
| free-text prose note | **29** |
| a tag from the five-word vocabulary | **5** |
| a category he invented on the spot (`jargon`) | 1 |

The five tags used: NO CLAIM, CAUSALITY ×2, DIRECTION, ACTOR.

**Two failures of the same taxonomy, from opposite directions.**

- **The machine over-uses CAUSALITY** — 7 of 9 on the synthetic test, including clean actor swaps.
- **The human under-uses everything.** Given five tags and 34 chances, he reached for prose 29 times.

And the one time he used ACTOR — *"key ACTOR I think is missing"* on Atlassian — he meant *"the
explanation is missing,"* which is CAUSALITY by the sheet's own definition. **The human and the
machine make the same mistake in opposite directions, which is what a bad vocabulary looks like from
both ends.**

He also invented a sixth category the five do not contain: **jargon** — *"way too many big words, I
don't think anyone knows what '106 antineutrino events at 5.9 sigma' means."* That is a real and
distinct failure: actor, direction, magnitude and causality all survive, and the reader still bounces
off the sentence.

**Recommendation, for the reviewer's call:**
1. Amendment 7's per-element bar is **unscoreable this round.** Do not report a number.
2. The rubric work required by delta 17 should be done **before** the next labeling pass, and the
   pass should be a forced choice — no free-text-only rows — or this will happen again.
3. Add **JARGON** as a sixth element, or fold it into a renamed CAUSALITY. The owner found it
   unprompted, which is the strongest evidence a category is real.

# 6. THE HEALTH BAR (amendment 21)

**Baseline for production is the SHIPPED column, because that is what the pipeline actually makes.**

| | value |
|---|---:|
| shipped baseline, owner-labeled, partials as landed | **80%** |
| shipped baseline, partials as failures | 73% |
| rewrite (what deliberate care achieves) | 94% |

**A bar set at 85% sits 5 points above current production.** On a 17–21 unit brief that is roughly
**2–4 units failing per night at the start** — enough to exercise the loop without swamping the
two-cycle cap, and reachable, since the rewrite hit 94% by hand.

**Proposed: 85%, phased.** Nights 1–7 the bar is *reported only* while unanimity-to-actuate is in
force; it becomes the health bar at night 8 when actuation relaxes to majority. 🔴 Owner signs the
number; derivation is this table.

**Two caveats that belong beside the number, not under it.** The baseline is one brief, one night,
n=15 scoreable shipped units. And it is a brief that ran ~1,870 words against a 1,300–1,600 spec whose
own budgets sum to 1,635 — an unusually loaded night. **Re-measure on the May archive era before the
number is frozen.**
