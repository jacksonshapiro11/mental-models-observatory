## SO WHERE DOES 90.3% COME FROM?

Two candidates remain, and only one is testable cheaply.

1. **Population.** The 342 graded units are light-brief units, most never flagged by anyone. Nothing
   surprising in most of them transmitting. This explains some of the gap and is not a defect.
2. **INPUT, not rubric and not grader — and this is the one worth testing.** 🔴 **This audit gave the
   grader a CLEANER TASK THAN PRODUCTION DOES.** It saw the logged so_what and three WHY strings and
   nothing else. In production the grader has the prose, the claims file, the readers' full
   CLAIM+WHY output and the whole night in front of it at once, and it grades the claim leg first.
   **A grader holding the article in mind can supply the missing half of a so_what without noticing
   — which is exactly the defect Ruling B named in the selection judge, arriving one layer down.**
   If that is what is happening, the fix is **isolation of the comparison**, not the quote-the-
   delivery revision. Untested. One production night re-graded both ways settles it.

**But two-clause structure alone does not predict failure.** `cc-2` 08-11 is also two-clause — "buys
a hostile advocate rather than a closing, **so** the reverse termination fee is the only certainty a
seller can purchase" — and scored 3/3. **The difference is what the second clause does. Where it is
the ACTIONABLE point, it arrives. Where it is an ANALYTICAL EXTENSION past the reader's takeaway, it
does not.** Readers carry what they would act on and drop what the writer found interesting about it.
